The pacifier features a soft, flexible round teat, a seamless one-piece construction and HEVEA's signature butterfly-shaped shield with playful ventilation openings. Why choose a round teat? The round teat, also known as a cherry-shaped teat, features a rounded tip and a classic, symmetrical shape. Its rounded design offers a familiar teat shape that some babies may prefer, particularly those accustomed to round bottle teats. Unlike an orthodontic teat, which has a flattened profile, the round teat maintains its rounded shape and can be positioned in either direction in your baby's mouth. Every baby is different, and choosing between a round, orthodontic or symmetrical teat often comes down to your little one's individual preference. Extra soft and flexible A small ventilation hole in the teat allows air to move in and out as your baby sucks, helping the teat gently compress and adapt to the sucking movement. This gives the teat its soft, flexible feel against your newborn's gums. Seamless one-piece design HEVEA pacifiers feature a seamless, one-piece construction without unnecessary joints or hard-to-reach crevices, making them easy to clean and care for. The signature butterfly-shaped shield is designed to fit around your baby's nose and cheeks, while four playful ventilation openings allow air to circulate around the mouth area. Natural or coloured – what's the difference? Whether you prefer the classic natural rubber shade or one of HEVEA's beautiful colours, both versions are made from natural rubber latex derived from the Hevea brasiliensis tree. The natural version showcases the characteristic shade of natural rubber, while our coloured pacifiers contain carefully selected colour pigments incorporated during manufacturing. According to our manufacturer's declaration, the colour pigments used in HEVEA's coloured pacifiers are listed under US FDA regulation 21 CFR §178.3297. Both versions retain the naturally soft and flexible characteristics of natural rubber. Care and replacement For hygiene reasons, HEVEA recommends replacing pacifiers every 6–8 weeks , depending on use and care. Always inspect the pacifier carefully before each use, pulling the teat in all directions. Discard it immediately at the first signs of damage or weakness, even if it has been used for less than six weeks. Always follow the cleaning and sterilisation instructions supplied with the product.
